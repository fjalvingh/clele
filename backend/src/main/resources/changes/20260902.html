Projects now hold their parts

A project used to walk a ladder of states — Planning, Building, Completed — and pulling parts out of stock was a separate chore, done one dialog at a time. That is gone. A project is now either active or cancelled, and the difference is a physical one: an active project holds its parts. Put a part on the list and it comes off the shelf then and there.

Cancelling a project gives every one of those parts back to the location it came from, while keeping a record of what the project needed. Reactivating it fetches them all out of stock again. If the shelf can no longer cover a line — the parts went into something else in the meantime — the line is filled as far as it goes and marked short on the project screen, rather than the whole thing refusing to happen.

A cancelled project accepts no changes at all until you reactivate it. It can also be deleted, which removes the project, its parts list and its imported BOM for good. The stock movements it caused stay in the ledger and go on naming it, so the history of your stock never develops a hole.

Returning parts as you go

Every line of a project's parts list has a Return button. It asks how many when the project holds more than one, and simply confirms when it holds one, then puts them back where they came from. The line stays on the list with what it needs intact, so it reads as short until you fetch the parts again.

Two lists, and only one of them is a BOM

"BOM" meant two different things and it was never clear which. From now on:

  • the project parts list is the project's own list of parts — what it needs and what it is holding;
  • the BOM is the file you upload from your EDA tool, and the screen where its lines are matched to parts.

The button at the end of the matching screen is now Apply to project parts list, and pressing it does what putting a part on the list does anywhere else: the matched lines go onto the parts list and come out of stock. Anything the shelf cannot cover is reported instead of silently skipped.
